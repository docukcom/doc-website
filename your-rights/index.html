Your right to choose, explained

General information about NHS rules in England, with links to the official text. It is not legal or medical advice.

What the right is

If your GP, dentist or optometrist refers you for consultant-led treatment, or to a mental health professional, you can usually choose which provider you go to for your first outpatient appointment, and the clinical team within it. The provider must have an NHS contract for that service. This includes many independent clinics that provide NHS services, not only NHS hospitals.

The NHS website calls this a legal right. If you are not offered a choice, it suggests asking your doctor why and saying you want to go through your options.

When it does not apply

According to the NHS Choice Framework, you cannot choose if you:

  • are already being treated for the condition and this is an onward referral
  • are using urgent, emergency or crisis services
  • need urgent treatment, for example an urgent referral for suspected cancer
  • are a prisoner, on temporary release, or held in other prescribed accommodation
  • are detained in hospital under the Mental Health Act 1983
  • are a serving member of the armed forces
  • are using maternity services

Your GP also has to agree that the referral is clinically appropriate.

If you wait more than 18 weeks

Treatment for non-urgent, consultant-led care should usually start within 18 weeks of referral. If you have waited, or will wait, longer than that, you can ask to be referred to another provider that can see you sooner.

There are exceptions. For example, this does not apply if you chose to wait, if waiting is in your clinical interest, if no other provider can start sooner, or if you are using maternity services. The Choice Framework lists them all.

Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland

The NHS Choice Framework applies to England. Health services in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own rules, so this right may not work the same way there. DOC will start with England only.

Where waiting times come from

NHS England publishes monthly referral to treatment statistics, with waiting times by specialty and by provider. The NHS My Planned Care website also shows average waits for many hospitals. These averages describe groups of patients. Your own wait can be shorter or longer, depending on how urgent your case is.
